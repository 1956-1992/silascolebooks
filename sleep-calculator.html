Wake up at the right end of a sleep cycle — free, from Silas Cole Books

How it works: sleep runs in cycles of about 90 minutes. Waking at the end of a cycle usually feels easier than waking in the middle of one. The times below count back (or forward) in full cycles, plus about 15 minutes to fall asleep. Most adults do best with 5 or 6 full cycles a night.

Why cycles matter more than hours

Eight hours of sleep that ends mid-cycle can leave you groggier than seven and a half hours that ends right at a cycle's close. Each cycle moves through light sleep, deep sleep, and dream sleep — and your body does different repair work in each stage. Timing your wake-up to a cycle boundary is the simplest free upgrade most people never make.
